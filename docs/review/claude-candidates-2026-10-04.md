## Needs a human expert

- `disease:HEXA|driven_by|mech:autophagy` (PMID:41460292): The quote only reports 'impaired autophagy in the brains of a mouse model of Tay-Sachs disease' (likely the Hexa-/-Neu3-/- double knockout). This shows association, not that the disease arises through autophagy. A person should decide whether secondary autophagy failure in lysosomal storage disease counts as driven_by.
- `disease:LZTR1|driven_by|mech:haploinsufficiency` (PMID:30442762): Lztr1 haploinsufficiency in mice 'recapitulates Noonan syndrome phenotypes', but in humans LZTR1 Noonan syndrome is dominant (missense) or recessive (biallelic loss), and heterozygous null carriers are generally not affected by Noonan syndrome. Whether haploinsufficiency drives the human disease is contested; a biochemist should decide.
- `disease:LZTR1|has_phenotype|phenotype:HP:0001249` (PMID:30368668): 'Five of the seven probands had short stature or intellectual disabilities' does not say how many, if any, had intellectual disability rather than short stature. The full text is needed.
- `disease:LZTR1|has_phenotype|phenotype:HP:0004322` (PMID:30368668): 'Five of the seven probands had short stature or intellectual disabilities' is a disjunction, so the quote does not say how many, if any, had short stature. Short stature is plausible in LZTR1 Noonan syndrome; check the full text or a second source.
- `disease:PTPN11|driven_by|mech:tor-signaling` (PMID:22058153, PMID:31722741): Contested and variant/phenotype-specific. The mouse Q510E study shows that mTOR drives HCM, but the human LEOPARD heart study found 'mTOR complex 1 pathway showed widely attenuated activity' ('a different role compared with animal models'). This is not a disease-wide driver for the PTPN11 umbrella.
- `disease:SCN1A|driven_by|mech:protein-destabilization` (PMID:25576396): The quote describes one folding-defective FHM3 NaV1.1 mutant that 'showed overall gain of function when rescued'. That is a variant-level folding defect with a 'differential pathomechanism', not clearly the umbrella's disease mechanism, and it sits against the existing contested GoF/LoF edges.
- `therapy:kv7-openers|targets|mech:loss-of-function` (PMID:27602407): Open-label series of 11 patients: EZO 'appeared ... potentially beneficial' for loss-of-function KCNQ2 variants. That describes response by variant class, not the drug acting on a mechanism. The generic 'Loss of function' node would also imply the drug applies to unrelated genes' LoF; a KCNQ2-scoped link may fit better.
- `therapy:sodium-channel-blockers|targets|mech:gain-of-function` (PMID:34431999, PMID:36636894): The quotes support SCBs working better for sodium-channel GoF variants (SCN8A cohort; SCN1A 'could ... counteract excessive channel function', hedged). But the generic GoF node (its summary is about SNARE vesicle fusion) would imply SCBs counteract GoF in any gene. A human should decide whether to scope it to sodium-channel GoF.
- `vg:CACNA1A:missense|has_effect|mech:loss-of-function` (PMID:31468518): G230V and I1357S missense variants 'result in loss-of-function effects', but the same paper (title) reports both gain- and loss-of-function de novo CACNA1A missense mutations. Direction is variant-specific, so a person should decide whether a group-level LoF edge (possibly alongside GoF) is appropriate.
- `vg:KCNQ2:missense|has_effect|mech:gain-of-function` (PMID:25740509, PMID:28139826): Gain of function holds only for a subset of KCNQ2 missense variants (voltage-sensor R201/R198, 'opposite to the loss-of-function effects produced by previously found mutations'). Most KCNQ2 missense variants are loss of function or dominant-negative, so a group-level edge would mislead unless it is marked as a subset or paired with a loss-of-function edge. The target's summary is also SNARE-specific.
- `vg:KCNQ2:missense|has_effect|mech:protein-destabilization` (PMID:34020651): The quote is about a single epilepsy-causing mutation ('this mutation impedes proper folding during translation') and is hedged. KCNQ2 missense variants vary in effect (loss of function, dominant negative, gain of function), so a group-wide misfolding edge needs a human to decide on scope.
- `vg:LZTR1:missense|has_effect|mech:protein-destabilization` (PMID:39140257): The variant-specific effect is unclear. 'accelerated protein degradation or mislocalization' is reported for LZTR1 LOF variants in isolated CALM, but the quote does not say these were missense variants, or which ones were degraded and which mislocalized.
- `vg:SCN2A:missense|has_effect|mech:gain-of-function` (PMID:28256214, PMID:38148154): SCN2A missense variants split by direction: infantile-seizure variants are 'predominantly missense, leading to a gain of function', but the second paper models a missense loss-of-function variant (R853Q), and autism variants are loss of function. Whether a group-level gain-of-function edge is acceptable without a loss-of-function counterpart needs a human decision.
- `vg:SCN2A:missense|has_effect|mech:loss-of-function` (PMID:38148154, PMID:41642117): SCN2A missense variants are bimodal: the quotes name both a GoF variant (R1882Q) and LoF variants (R853Q, L1650P). A blanket missense→loss-of-function edge without a matching gain-of-function edge would mislead, so the edge needs a human decision on how to represent it.
- `vg:SCN8A:missense|has_effect|mech:gain-of-function` (PMID:30615093): The quote is variant-specific: R1872W showed 'clear gain-of-function' changes, but E1483K 'showed no significant biophysical changes'. SCN8A missense variants include both gain and loss of function, and the mechanism class is already flagged as disagreeing with G2P, so a human should decide.
- `vg:SCN8A:missense|has_effect|mech:loss-of-function` (PMID:30615093): The direction is variant-specific. R1620L (ID/autism without epilepsy) 'reduced Na+ current density', but most SCN8A epilepsy missense variants are gain-of-function, so a group-level missense->LOF edge from one variant would mislead. A human should decide the scope (perhaps a variant-level edge).

## Rejected

- `disease:HRAS|driven_by|mech:autophagy` (PMID:34508588): The quote says accelerated autophagic flux in patient fibroblasts contributes 'to the increased energetic expenditure in CS'. That is a downstream cellular-metabolic finding, not a statement that Costello syndrome arises through autophagy, so driven_by overstates it.
- `disease:HRAS|driven_by|mech:glucose-transmembrane-transport` (PMID:34508588): The quote reports constitutive GLUT4 translocation and accelerated glycolysis in Costello patient fibroblasts, a downstream metabolic phenotype; it does not state that Costello syndrome arises through glucose transmembrane transport, so driven_by overstates it.
- `disease:KRAS|has_phenotype|phenotype:HP:0004322` (PMID:16474405): The quote describes Noonan syndrome in general ('characterized by short stature...'), a broader group than KRAS-related disease. It is not an observation in KRAS mutation carriers.
- `disease:NF1|has_phenotype|phenotype:HP:0001263` (PMID:34346503): The quote attributes developmental delay to three NF1 subjects who also carried a co-occurring PTPN11 gain-of-function variant, presented as a modifier of severe expression. It does not support the phenotype for NF1-related disorders as such.
- `disease:RAF1|driven_by|mech:positive-regulation-of-ras-signaling` (PMID:17603483): The quote is about PTPN11, SOS1 and KRAS mutations ('Increased RAS signaling owing to PTPN11, SOS1 and KRAS mutations...'), not RAF1. Wrong gene. RAF1 also acts downstream of RAS rather than regulating Ras signal transduction.
- `disease:SMPD1|driven_by|mech:regulation-of-synaptic-plasticity` (PMID:40609755): The evidence is a mouse neuron finding ('impaired presynaptic plasticity in acid sphingomyelinase deficient neurons'). It does not state that the disease arises through dysregulated synaptic plasticity, so it is at most a downstream cellular effect, not a driven_by mechanism.
- `disease:SOS1|driven_by|mech:mapk-cascade` (PMID:17143282): Wrong gene: the quote says increased RAS-MAPK signalling 'due to PTPN11 and KRAS mutations causes 50% of cases of Noonan syndrome'. It says nothing about SOS1.
- `disease:SOS1|has_phenotype|phenotype:HP:0010310` (PMID:33219052): The quote is about Noonan syndrome as a whole ('in ~20% of the cases, is associated with severe lymphatic disorders, including chylothorax'), not about SOS1-related disease. The title hints at an SOS1 case, but no quoted sentence ties chylothorax to SOS1.
- `gene:LZTR1|participates_in|mech:autophagy` (PMID:31337872): Speculative inference only: ubiquitin chain types 'suggesting its possible involvement in autophagy'. The quote does not show that LZTR1 takes part in autophagy.
- `therapy:hsct-krabbe|developed_for|disease:ARSA` (PMID:42764516): Wrong therapy node. The source is the Krabbe-specific HSCT node (supplies galactocerebrosidase), while the quote is about HSCT for MLD ('MLD ... can be treated with HSCT'). This needs a separate or generic HSCT-for-MLD node; it does not belong on the Krabbe one.
- `therapy:sodium-channel-blockers|targets|mech:protein-destabilization` (PMID:25576396): The quote concerns phenytoin alone acting as a pharmacological chaperone for folding-defective NaV1.1 mutants in cells. Generalising this to the whole sodium-channel-blocker class is unsupported. It is also clinically misleading, since this class is usually avoided in SCN1A/Dravet.
