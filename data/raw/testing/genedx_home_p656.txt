Genetic testing services
Explore GeneDx's genetic testing services for rare and inherited conditions.
Exome sequencing
Exome sequencing is comprehensive genetic testing that analyzes protein-coding regions of the genome to help identify genetic variants associated with rare and inherited conditions. Rapid exome sequencing options are available for time-sensitive clinical situations.
Learn about whole exome sequencing
Genome sequencing
Genome sequencing is broad genomic testing that analyzes both coding and noncoding regions to help uncover genetic causes of disease that may be missed by more targeted approaches. Rapid and ultra-rapid genome sequencing options are available for critically ill patients.
Learn about whole genome sequencing
Trio testing
Trio testing analyzes a patient alongside biological parents to help improve variant interpretation and increase diagnostic yield for complex or rare genetic conditions.
Learn about trio testing
Targeted variant testing
Confirm or evaluate a known genetic variant in a patient or family member. Ideal for follow-up testing when a disease-causing variant has already been identified.
Learn about targeted variant testing

Common questions about genetic testing
Get answers to questions about genetic testing, its benefits, and more.
See all FAQs
How do I get genetic testing?
Genetic testing is typically ordered by a healthcare provider based on a patient’s symptoms and medical history. Providers can explore available tests and ordering options through GeneDx, while patients and families can learn how to get started and what to expect during the process.
What diseases can be detected through genetic testing?
Genetic testing can help identify genetic causes of many rare and inherited conditions, including neurodevelopmental disorders, epilepsy, movement disorders, metabolic conditions, cardiac conditions and other complex or unexplained symptoms. GeneDx specializes in exome and genome sequencing, which can analyze many genes at once to help clinicians find answers for patients with suspected genetic conditions.
What does GeneDx test for?
GeneDx provides genetic testing and genomic sequencing to help diagnose rare and complex conditions. Our testing supports patients with developmental delay, intellectual disability, epilepsy, autism spectrum disorder, congenital anomalies, and other inherited conditions across pediatric and adult populations.
Who should consider genetic testing for rare disease?
Genetic testing may be appropriate for individuals with unexplained developmental delays, intellectual disability, epilepsy, congenital anomalies, or other suspected genetic conditions. Healthcare providers can determine when testing is appropriate based on clinical presentation.Learn more.
What are the benefits of exome and genome sequencing?
Whole exome and whole genome sequencing analyze thousands of genes at once, increasing the likelihood of identifying the underlying cause of a condition. These tests can help deliver faster, more accurate diagnoses and inform medical management and care decisions.
How can clinicians order genetic testing?
Clinicians can order genetic testing through GeneDx by selecting the appropriate test and submitting a requisition form. Our team provides support throughout the ordering process, including test selection, specimen requirements, and result interpretation.

GeneDx specializes in whole exome sequencing and whole genome sequencing to help clinicians find answers for patients with suspected genetic conditions.
What does GeneDx test for?
GeneDx offers genetic testing for a wide range of rare and inherited conditions. Our testing includes whole exome sequencing, whole genome sequencing, trio testing, prenatal genetic testing, targeted testing, and other specialized tests designed to help identify genetic causes of disease and support more informed clinical care.
What is genetic testing?
Genetic testing analyzes DNA to help identify genetic changes that may contribute to rare and inherited conditions. Results can help guide diagnosis, care decisions, and treatment planning for patients and families.
How does genetic testing work?
Genetic testing typically starts with a blood sample or cheek swab collected by a
healthcare provider or through an at-home collection kit. Samples are analyzed in a
laboratory, and results are reviewed by clinical experts to help guide next steps in care.
What conditions can genetic testing help diagnose?
Genetic testing can help diagnose a wide range of rare and inherited conditions, including developmental delay, intellectual disability, epilepsy, autism spectrum disorder, congenital anomalies, neurologic conditions, and other complex genetic disorders.
What are the benefits of exome and genome sequencing?
Exome and genome sequencing provide broad genetic analysis that can help uncover causes of rare and inherited conditions that may be missed by more targeted testing approaches. These tests may support earlier diagnosis, more informed care decisions, and improved treatment planning.
Does insurance cover genetic testing?
Many insurance plans, including commercial insurance, Medicaid, Medicare, and Tricare, may cover genetic testing when it is considered medically necessary. Coverage requirements vary by plan and clinical situation. GeneDx works with ordering providers to support insurance authorization and reimbursement processes when applicable.
When is genetic testing recommended?
Genetic testing may be recommended when a patient has symptoms or medical findings that suggest a possible genetic condition. Exome and genome sequencing are increasingly recommended as first-line tests for many children with developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism, epilepsy, congenital anomalies, and other suspected rare diseases.
How do I get genetic testing done?
Genetic testing is typically ordered by a healthcare provider based on medical history, symptoms, and clinical presentation. Patients and families can explore testing options and next steps on the Patients page, while healthcare providers can browse tests and ordering information on the How to order page.
What is genetic testing?
Genetic testing looks at our genes which are the instructions that tell our bodies how to develop and function. Sometimes changes in our genes (also called genetic variants) cause our bodies to grow or develop differently than expected. Genetic testing may be able to find these gene changes. That knowledge can help determine:
‍
how to best manage or treat a condition or prevent complications related to a genetic diagnosis
what to expect for the future
which additional resources and support may help
